Method and sources

Final demand vs inputs. PPI final demand for construction measures the price a contractor charges the owner for finished work: materials, labor, equipment, overhead and profit. Input PPIs measure material costs only. Use final demand, not inputs, to represent whole-building cost change. A large input increase often shows up as a much smaller final-demand increase.

Why quarterly. BLS models the monthly values and corrects them with a contractor survey in January, April, July and October. Each correction revises that month and the two before it. Quarters here end in those survey months: Feb–Apr is Q1, May–Jul is Q2, Aug–Oct is Q3, and Nov–Jan is Q4 of the prior year. Each quarter is the average of its three monthly index values.

Read complete quarters only. Months after the last survey are modeled and can change sign when the survey lands. Per Ed Zarenski's analysis, most of a quarter's change has typically posted in the survey month, and the correction has reversed the modeled direction in several recent quarters. The quarter in progress is shown faded and is left out of the summary.

Measures. QoQ = quarter average vs the prior quarter. YoY = quarter average vs the same quarter a year earlier. Annualized = QoQ compounded over four quarters. Trade average = simple average of the four trade-contractor changes.

Method follows Ed Zarenski's guidance on PPI final demand at edzarenski.com. For internal planning; not a forecast.
